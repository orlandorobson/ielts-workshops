# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Fictional learner models and feedback

Version 1.0 · 3 November 2026  
Scope: all nine canonical workshop questions; eight new models plus the accepted Aisha model.

All writers and responses are fictional teaching constructions informed by authentic learner patterns. They are not untouched real submissions. Scores are provisional teaching estimates, not official IELTS marks or full Writing-test results. No actual completion times are claimed.

The [official Task 2 criteria](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf) inform the four-criterion review. Each criterion is considered separately. A 5.5 task estimate here is the arithmetic mean of different whole-band criterion estimates; there is no invented 5.5 descriptor or half-band criterion checklist. Each displayed task estimate equals its criterion mean, so no rounding convention is assumed.

The new Band 6 responses are criteria-informed constructions. They are not authenticated by the current mostly lower-band student corpus. Stronger submissions will inform later calibration.

Teacher copy: this document includes all answers. An item is no longer unseen transfer material for a learner once its response has been shown.

## Salma — Estimated Task 2 Band 5.5

**Question:** WT2-W01 · **Model:** WT2-M02 · **Words:** 268

### Canonical question

Some people believe that public libraries should spend more money on printed books than on digital resources.

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Salma's response

Public libraries have to choose how they spend their money because they cannot buy everything. I partly agree that printed books should get more money. They are still useful, but the digital side matters too.

One reason is that not everyone can read from a phone or a computer. For older people the screen can be difficult and also some children do not have their own device. If the library only buy digital books, these people cannot use them in the same way. My grandmother like to borrow books about cooking and she would not know how to download one. Also a printed book can stay in the library for years and many people can borrow it. This make the money useful. Of course the children books can be damaged, so they need replacing too.

However, digital resources are useful when people cannot going to the library. A person who finish work late can find a book at home instead of waiting until the weekend. This is a good service and libraries should provide it. Digital books also do not need shelves. Some libraries have a small space and there is not enough place for all the books. But I still think printed books need more money because a library is a place where people come to read books. If everything become online they may not come anymore and the library will lose its importance.

In conclusion, printed books should have the bigger part of the money, although some money should go to digital resources. Both are useful but printed books are the main thing in a library.

### Examiner-style feedback

**Task Response — 6**

Salma addresses the spending comparison and supports a qualified position. Her final reason becomes circular: printed books deserve priority because libraries contain books. Explaining which users would lose most from reduced print spending would strengthen her judgement.

**Coherence and Cohesion — 6**

Her two main paragraphs have distinct purposes and the argument is generally followable. The damaged-books point interrupts the access argument. Keeping that detail brief, or linking it to the cost comparison, would improve progression.

**Lexical Resource — 5**

Salma has enough vocabulary to communicate her ideas, but relies on general expressions such as “make the money useful” and “the main thing.” More precise everyday phrases about costs and access would clarify her argument.

**Grammatical Range and Accuracy — 5**

She attempts conditions and relative clauses, but basic verb patterns remain unreliable: “the library only buy” and “cannot going.” Controlling present-tense agreement and the verb after modal verbs would improve accuracy.

## Omar — Estimated Task 2 Band 6.0

**Question:** WT2-W02 · **Model:** WT2-M03 · **Words:** 321

### Canonical question

Some people think public parks should mainly provide quiet natural spaces, while others think they should include more sports facilities.

Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

### Omar's response

There are different ideas about what a park should offer. Some people want a quiet place with trees, while others prefer places to exercise. I think parks should keep their natural areas, but there is also a need for some sports facilities, especially in crowded cities.

People who prefer quiet parks have a good reason. Many residents live near busy roads and they hear traffic for most of the day. A park gives them somewhere to sit without this pressure. For example, an older person may go there to walk slowly or meet a friend. If a football pitch is built in the same area, the shouting and balls can disturb them. Trees are important for the city as well, and replacing a large green area with hard surfaces would make the park less attractive. However, not all sports facilities need to take a big space.

On the other hand, sports areas can encourage people to be active without paying for a gym. This is useful for families who cannot afford memberships. A few exercise machines and a court could be used by different ages, so the park have more benefits for the community. In my area there is a walking track beside a small sports ground and both are usually busy in the evening. This shows that the two uses can work together. The problem is when too many people use it and there is no place for others. The council should organise this better, although it may be difficult in a small park.

Another issue is who looks after the equipment. Sometimes it is broken and nobody use it, which is a waste. Providing it is not enough if the council then forget about it.

Overall, I would not turn parks into sports centres, but leaving out sport completely is also a loss. A mixture is the best option, with the quiet part protected from the more noisy activities.

### Examiner-style feedback

**Task Response — 6**

Omar explains both views and maintains a balanced opinion. His point that the council should “organise this better” is underdeveloped. A practical explanation of how limited space could be shared would make his recommendation more convincing.

**Coherence and Cohesion — 6**

Omar generally progresses clearly between the two views. However, the late maintenance paragraph starts another issue without linking it to his recommendation. Bringing that concern into the sports discussion would make the ending more focused.

**Lexical Resource — 6**

Omar uses relevant vocabulary such as “hard surfaces” and “memberships.” Some wording is less precise: “take a big space” and “a loss.” More natural expressions for space and missed opportunities would strengthen this otherwise understandable language.

**Grammatical Range and Accuracy — 6**

He combines simple and complex sentences, with meaning generally clear. Errors remain in “the park have” and “more noisy.” Checking agreement and comparative forms would improve control without making the sentences longer.

## Faisal — Estimated Task 2 Band 5.0

**Question:** WT2-W03 · **Model:** WT2-M04 · **Words:** 267

### Canonical question

In some countries, people are increasingly using cards and mobile phones instead of cash to pay for goods and services.

What are the advantages and disadvantages of this development?

### Faisal's response

Nowadays people use their phone for everything and paying is one of these things. In some shops you can pay by card or phone and you do not need cash. There are good and bad sides.

First of all, it is easy and fast because people always have the phone with them. When I go to buy something I just use my phone and finish, I don't need to look for money in my pocket. Also you can see how much money you spend because the bank send you a message. This is good to know your money and management it. For example, you know what you buy and how much you pay for it. Another advantage is cash can lost but the phone have password so people cannot take your money easily. This make people feel safe when they go out.

But there is bad things also. Sometimes the phone battery finish and you cannot pay. This happened to my friend and he ask me to pay for him. Also old people do not know how to use all these things. My father use a card but he still like cash because he can see the money. In addition, when you pay by phone you do not feel you spend much and you buy more things. ### Examiner-style feedback

**Task Response — 5**

Faisal discusses benefits and drawbacks, with a relevant battery example. Several other points are asserted rather than explained, particularly phone security. Explaining one realistic situation would provide stronger support than repeating that payment is easy.

**Coherence and Cohesion — 5**

The advantages and disadvantages are separated, but ideas accumulate within each paragraph. His ending moves towards general saving advice. Grouping related points and returning to the payment change would make the overall direction clearer.

**Lexical Resource — 5**

Faisal communicates everyday experiences, but relies heavily on “things” and uses awkward expressions such as “know your money” and “battery finish.” Learning precise phrases for tracking spending and a flat battery would improve clarity.

**Grammatical Range and Accuracy — 5**

Reasons and examples are attempted, but errors such as “cash can lost” and “the bank send” are frequent. Clearer sentence endings and reliable verb forms would make his ideas easier to read.

## Rania — Estimated Task 2 Band 6.0

**Question:** WT2-W04 · **Model:** WT2-M05 · **Words:** 262

### Canonical question

Some museums allow visitors to enter free of charge rather than charging an admission fee.

Do the advantages of this approach outweigh the disadvantages?

### Rania's response

Museums can teach people about history and culture, but some visitors are discouraged by the entrance price. In my view, the benefits of free entry are greater than the disadvantages, although finding enough money for the museum is a real difficulty.

The main benefit is that more people have the opportunity to visit. A family with several children have a large cost when everyone has to pay. They may decide to spend the money on food or another activity instead. If entry is free, parents can bring their children for a short visit and return another day. Children does not always want a long visit. It could make them more interested in their country's history, which is important for the next generation.

The disadvantage is the loss of income. Museums need staff, cleaning and protection for old objects. Without ticket money, these expenses does not disappear. The government could pay more, but there are also schools and hospitals which need support. Some museums can earn money from a cafe or a gift shop instead. However, visitors may bring their own food and not buy anything, so this is not always a reliable solution. Rich companies could sponsor the museum and they can easily cover the missing amount. This also give the companies a better image in society.

In conclusion, I think access for everyone is more important than collecting entrance fees. The financial problem needs attention, but a museum should mainly be an educational place. Free visits would help it reach more people, even if it has to depend on outside support.

### Examiner-style feedback

**Task Response — 6**

Rania makes an outweigh judgement and develops the access benefit. Her funding solution is too confident: companies may not “easily cover the missing amount.” Explaining the limits of sponsorship would make her comparison more convincing.

**Coherence and Cohesion — 6**

Her argument has a clear overall direction, but the finance paragraph moves through government support, shop income and sponsorship quickly. Developing the connection between these options would reduce the list-like progression.

**Lexical Resource — 6**

Rania uses appropriate terms including “loss of income,” “expenses” and “sponsor.” “Collecting entrance fees” oversimplifies the museum's financial purpose. More precise wording about maintaining services would improve the comparison.

**Grammatical Range and Accuracy — 6**

She uses a mixture of sentence forms with generally clear meaning. Agreement slips remain, including “these expenses does not disappear,” alongside repeated use of “would.” More consistent agreement and greater structural flexibility would strengthen control.

## Maha — Estimated Task 2 Band 5.5

**Question:** WT2-W05 · **Model:** WT2-M06 · **Words:** 336

### Canonical question

Large numbers of tourists visit some small towns and villages.

What problems can this cause for local residents, and what measures could be taken to address these problems?

### Maha's response

Tourism is important for small towns because it brings customers, but residents can have a difficult life when too many visitors arrive. The main problems are traffic and rubbish. I think local authorities and tourists both have responsibility to reduce these problems.

During holidays, roads in a village can become full of cars and residents cannot reach their houses easily. Last summer we visited a small village and there were cars parked outside people's gates. One man was trying to get his car out but the visitors were not there. This situation make residents angry even if they welcome tourism normally. Also restaurants become crowded and there is rubbish near the picnic areas. The rubbish damages the environment and sometimes animals eat it. The shop owners should benefit from visitors but they have to clean more and this take their time. Prices can increase too, especially rent, and young people cannot find an affordable house. A person who works in the village may have to live outside it. Then they need to drive back to work, which adds more traffic again. So one problem can make another problem worse and it is not easy for the local people.

To solve the traffic problem, the council can make a parking area outside the village and use small buses to bring people inside. This would leave the narrow streets free for residents. But the parking should not be expensive or people will continue parking anywhere. Also there should be more bins and regular collection because a bin is not useful when it is already full. Tourists must respect the place and not leave their rubbish. There should be information and fines for this. Some people do not care even when they see a sign so the rules need someone to check them.

In conclusion, visitors can cause problems with parking and the environment, although the village need their spending. Better organisation can reduce these problems and residents should be asked what they need before more tourists are invited.

### Examiner-style feedback

**Task Response — 6**

Maha identifies relevant resident problems and connects parking and waste measures to them. Housing costs are introduced but then left aside. Either developing that problem and a response, or omitting it, would improve coverage and focus.

**Coherence and Cohesion — 5**

Her main sections are recognisable, but the problems paragraph jumps from blocked gates to animals, shopkeepers and housing. Selecting fewer problems and giving each a clear sequence would make the discussion easier to follow.

**Lexical Resource — 6**

Maha uses helpful topic language such as “affordable house,” “regular collection” and “narrow streets.” Expressions including “make a parking area” and “rules need someone to check them” lack precision. More natural enforcement and parking phrases would help.

**Grammatical Range and Accuracy — 5**

She attempts conditions and explanations, but agreement is repeatedly insecure: “This situation make,” “this take” and “the village need.” Controlling these recurring singular-subject patterns would improve accuracy across the essay.

## Nasser — Estimated Task 2 Band 5.5

**Question:** WT2-W07 · **Model:** WT2-M07 · **Words:** 301

### Canonical question

Many people living in cities experience high levels of noise in their daily lives.

What are the main causes of this problem, and what could be done to reduce it?

### Nasser's response

Noise is a serious problem for people in cities. It can affect their sleep and make them feel stressed, especially when it continues at night. Traffic and construction are two main causes. There are ways to improve the situation, but I think people also need to change their behaviour.

The first cause is the number of vehicles that moving on the road. In busy areas drivers use their horns even when there is nowhere for the other car to go. Motorcycles can also make a very loud sound, and these noises continues for many hours. People who living above the shops have to hear it every day. It is not only during working hours because deliveries sometimes come late at night. This means the residents do not get a proper rest even when they are inside their own home.

Construction is another cause because cities expanding all the time. Machines and drilling can continue from early morning, so children and older people cannot sleep. Workers wants to finish the building quickly but they should consider the neighbours. When a building near my house was repaired, the noise was so strong that we cannot hear the television. Closing the windows did not help much. This shows how serious the problem is for families.

For solutions, the government should introduce strict regulations and punish people who make too much noise. There should be more awareness in schools because children are the future drivers and workers. People must understand that everyone have a right to live in a peaceful environment. If people respect each other, there would be less noise and the city will become more comfortable.

In conclusion, traffic and construction disturb urban residents and affect their health. The authorities should take this seriously and people should be more responsible about their actions.

### Examiner-style feedback

**Task Response — 5**

Nasser explains traffic and construction causes with relevant detail. The solutions receive less development: “strict regulations” does not identify what should be controlled. A specific rule and how it reduces noise would improve task coverage.

**Coherence and Cohesion — 6**

His sections follow a clear causes-to-solutions sequence. However, several sentences repeat how serious the noise is, leaving less room for the final section. Reducing repetition would allow a better balance between the two tasks.

**Lexical Resource — 6**

Nasser uses relevant expressions such as “deliveries,” “regulations” and “peaceful environment.” Some wording remains general, particularly “take this seriously.” Naming the intended restriction would make his vocabulary more precise and useful.

**Grammatical Range and Accuracy — 5**

Nasser attempts explanations and conditions, but several clauses are incomplete, including “cities expanding all the time” and “people who living.” Making the subject and full verb visible in each clause would improve sentence control.

## Layla — Estimated Task 2 Band 6.0

**Question:** WT2-W08 · **Model:** WT2-M08 · **Words:** 280

### Canonical question

In many countries, family members are eating meals together less often than in the past.

Why is this happening, and what effects can it have on family life?

### Layla's response

Eating together used to be a normal part of family life, but it is becoming less regular in many homes. I think different schedules are the main reason. This change can give family members more freedom, but it can also reduce the time they spend talking to each other.

One reason is that work and study do not finish at the same time. A parent may come home late while the children have already eaten before their evening activities. Waiting for everyone could mean eating very late, so separate meals become the easier choice. Food delivery also make this arrangement possible. Each person can order what they want instead of waiting for a meal to be cooked for the whole family. In my family we sometimes do this at weekends, although we are all in the same house. It is convenient, but we spend more money than when we cook together.

The main effect is that families can lose a regular chance to communicate. During a meal, parents may hear about a child's day or notice that something is wrong. If everybody eats alone, these small conversations might not happen. This do not mean that families stop caring about each other, but they have to find another time to talk. A meal is better because everyone should listen. Young children also learns good manners from eating together, and this is something they cannot learn properly on their own.

In conclusion, busy schedules and the convenience of separate food are making shared meals less common. The effect on family relationships can be negative, so families should try to have a meal together when possible, even if it is not every day.

### Examiner-style feedback

**Task Response — 6**

Layla addresses reasons and effects with relevant explanation. Her claims that meals are necessarily better for listening and that manners cannot be learnt alone are too broad. Qualifying these claims would make her support more convincing.

**Coherence and Cohesion — 6**

Her paragraphs generally progress clearly. The delivery paragraph ends with extra spending, but that consequence is not connected to the later discussion of family life. A clearer link would help the reader follow its contribution.

**Lexical Resource — 6**

Layla communicates naturally with phrases such as “different schedules” and “regular chance.” “The convenience of separate food” is less precise. She could distinguish separate mealtimes from ordering individual meals more clearly.

**Grammatical Range and Accuracy — 6**

Layla uses simple and complex sentences with generally clear meaning, but agreement remains inconsistent: “delivery also make” and “This do not mean.” More reliable verb forms, especially when the subject changes, would strengthen her control.
