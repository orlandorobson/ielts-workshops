# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Model-answer authoring guide

Version 1.1 · 3 November 2026  
Status: working authoring standard  
Scope: realistic learner models for the canonical Task 2 workshop bank

## 1. Purpose

Create believable learner writing that students can investigate, understand and improve. The intended reaction is: **“I could write something like this, and I can see my next step.”**

Models must represent imperfect reasoning, organisation and language. They must not be expert essays with grammar and spelling errors inserted afterwards.

**Write from the learner forward, never from the expert backward.**

This guide governs authoring and review. It does not authorise publication, deployment or changes to the submission portal.

## 2. Evidence base and current ceiling

The current evidence consists of six essays from four writers. Original student responses were separated from teacher feedback, correction and reconstruction. Two writers have repeat submissions on different topics; these are useful comparisons, not controlled evidence of a learning effect.

The supplied reports mainly estimate criterion performance around Bands 4–5, with one stronger lexical estimate. These are report estimates, not newly validated scores. A stronger vocabulary criterion does not establish a stronger overall essay band.

**Current authorisation:** the user has explicitly requested fictional models spanning estimated Bands 5, 5.5 and 6, including criteria-informed Band 6 constructions before stronger authentic submissions arrive. These are permitted as clearly labelled constructed teaching responses, never as authentic Band 6 student submissions. Stronger authentic writing will later help test and refine them. Fluency, length, sophisticated vocabulary or a single successful paragraph is insufficient for a band judgement.

Current developmental stages describe changes in capabilities; they are not a substitute band scale. Do not invent a separate checklist for Band 5.5 or promise a score increase from a particular correction.

## 4. Three distinct kinds of teaching text

| Kind | Purpose | Labelling rule |
|---|---|---|
| Authentic extract | Show exactly what a student produced | Preserve wording; identify provenance internally |
| Constructed learner composite | Create a new response using observed patterns and, where suitable, borrowed fragments | State that it is a constructed teaching response; never attribute it to a real student |
| Short skill demonstration | Make one successful writing decision easy to see | Label the demonstrated skill; do not present it as an authentic whole-essay band exemplar |

A short successful demonstration may be controlled and clear. A full learner composite must retain believable limitations in all four criteria, with different severity and combinations across writers. Whole-essay Band 6 constructions require a complete four-criterion review and explicit fictional status. Band 6-plus authentic claims still require authentic evidence; models above estimated Band 6 are outside the current authorisation.

## 5. Choose a writer profile before writing

Select one main evidence-grounded profile. Add only compatible secondary behaviours. These describe writing in this corpus, not permanent identities or universal band types.

| Profile | Preserve the capability | Preserve the limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Idea-rich, weakly bounded | Numerous ideas, examples, comparisons and attempts at explanation | Sentence chains, unstable forms, drift from the exact decision |
| Concrete, unevenly connected | Explicit opinion and recognisable everyday examples; occasional successful explanation | Reference drift, general placeholders, abrupt or incomplete closure |
| Structurally familiar, thinly developed | Paragraphs, stance markers and recognisable essay signals | Restatement, incomplete examples, mismatch between opening frame and actual position |
| Conceptually ambitious, overextended | Wider vocabulary, qualification and individual/social perspectives | Compressed causal chains, unsupported escalation and phrase instability |

Do not combine every weakness into one response. Do not make all learners sound alike. Do not assume limited grammatical control means limited thought or a tiny vocabulary.

## 6. Behaviours to capture

### Answering a neighbouring question

Relevant topic knowledge can grow into an essay that does not quite answer the specified comparison or judgement. Preserve that distinction. Do not quietly supply exact task alignment in every paragraph.

### Signalling without completing the relationship

A writer may use “For example” before a restatement, or “In other words” before another vague claim. Diagnose what follows the signal, not the presence of a connector alone.

### Repetition versus accumulation

These are different profiles. One writer says the same thing repeatedly; another keeps adding ideas without finishing them. Do not give both a generic “add more detail” treatment.

### Specific examples with missing links

A concrete situation may be relevant without explaining why it supports the claim. Increased specificity need not produce complete reasoning immediately.

### Vocabulary beyond grammatical control

Useful topic words may appear in the wrong form or an unusual combination. Preserve lexical ambition, approximation and uneven control without scattering arbitrary errors.

### Complex relationships before controlled sentences

Condition, cause, comparison and concession may be attempted inside faulty sentences. Do not reduce lower-control writing to uniformly short, simple statements.

### Reference and register drift

The writer may move between a general group and conversational “you,” or lose control of singular/plural reference. Tie these shifts to the writer's train of thought rather than inserting them randomly.

### Successful islands

One clear, well-developed sentence may coexist with much weaker passages. Preserve it. An unusually fluent phrase does not establish its origin or prove independent control of the whole response. Do not infer copying or AI authorship without evidence.

### Unsupported certainty

A plausible concern may become a sweeping prediction without an explanatory chain. Represent this as a reasoning limitation, not as a factual claim the workshop endorses. Avoid reproducing a stereotype merely to make a model feel authentic.

## 7. Borrowing directly and preserving provenance

1. Select evidence-register entries that support the intended profile.
2. Keep verbatim fragments unchanged, including errors. Whitespace may be normalised for layout; record that choice.
3. Mark any topic substitution, joining, extension or other change as adaptation.
4. Record which passages are verbatim, adapted or newly composed.
5. Keep real students' identities separate from fictional model identities.
6. Never imply that a student wrote a new composite, a different prompt response or an upper-band version.
7. Use the original prompt context when interpreting an extract. A sentence's usefulness depends on the job it was meant to perform.

Borrowing a pattern does not require copying a phrase. A near-literal patchwork of several students can be less believable than a new response built around one coherent profile.

## 8. Authoring workflow

### A. Bind the task

Record the canonical question ID and version. Copy its wording exactly. Privately map its required actions and limits, without turning that map into an expert plan supplied invisibly to the learner model.

### B. Set the capability profile

Describe what this writer can already do and what they cannot yet sustain in each criterion. Use plain descriptions before numbers.

### C. Make a plausible learner plan

Choose ideas the profile could generate. Allow imperfect selection, uneven development or a missed demand where appropriate. Do not optimise the plan and then damage only the language.

### D. Draft from that plan

Let the writer's decisions produce the text. Preserve natural repetition, jumps, ambitious attempts and occasional success. Do not impose an error quota, equal error spacing or a compulsory sentence count.

### E. Check task conditions

Record actual word count and whether the text is a full essay or a partial teaching response. Full models should normally meet the 250-word minimum; an intentionally short response must be labelled and discussed honestly. Do not invent a completion time or claim that a synthetic essay was written under real test conditions.

### F. Audit realism

Run the checks in section 11. Rewrite the model if it depends on invisible expert selection or implausibly uniform weakness.

### G. Assess the completed script

Use the official Task 2 descriptors and evidence from the entire response. Do not write to a predetermined band or automatically inherit a source student's estimate. Keep any assessment provisional and clearly labelled as an estimated teaching assessment. Estimated Bands 5, 5.5 and 6 are authorised for constructed models. Justify each complete response against the criteria, including its weaknesses; do not describe the estimates as externally validated.

### H. Write feedback and a next action

Feedback must describe the actual model and lead to a manageable learner revision. Keep extensive correction optional.

## 9. Build a developmental ladder without pretending every skill rises together

For the current corpus, use observable movements such as:

- Making an exact position visible while language remains unstable.
- Marking sentence boundaries while paragraph reasoning remains thin.
- Introducing a concrete example while its explanatory link remains partly implicit.
- Connecting one claim and consequence while other ideas remain underdeveloped.
- Stabilising a useful phrase while other word forms remain unreliable.

These are candidate changes, not mandatory stages for every writer. A later model may keep a weakness, improve only locally or introduce a new error while attempting something more demanding.

When illustrating development, record what changed and what stayed imperfect. A spelling-cleaned version is not automatically a stronger Task Response. More paragraphs are not automatically stronger coherence. A revised sentence is not evidence of independent transfer.

Do not attribute constructed developmental versions to a real student's progress. Distinguish supported revision, independent writing and a newly constructed illustration.

## 10. Feedback format matching Task 1

### Quick four-criterion view

Provide short, script-specific comments on:

- **Task Response:** exact coverage, position, relevance, explanation and support.
- **Coherence and Cohesion:** organisation, progression, paragraph contribution and relationships.
- **Lexical Resource:** vocabulary range, precision, appropriacy, word formation and spelling.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** structural choices, control, recurring errors and their effect on understanding.

Start with a genuine success where appropriate. Name the limiting feature and use brief exact evidence. Different models should receive different feedback, not the same template with names changed.

### Optional closer look and correction

Separate missing reasoning from language mistakes. Where meaning is uncertain, offer interpretations or a clarification question rather than silently deciding what the writer intended. Mark substantive changes to an argument as changes, not grammatical correction.

### One useful revision

Choose one or two high-value priorities. Ask the learner to improve something in their own writing. Do not make an expert rewrite the default result.

Criterion estimates, if used, require a written rationale and the label **estimated teaching assessment, not an official IELTS result**. Never attach bands to isolated sentences or equate Cool/Spicy/Splat with IELTS bands.

## 11. Mandatory quality-control review

A model is ready for use only when all of these checks pass:

- **Invisible-competence check:** mentally repair grammar and spelling. Does a believable learner's imperfect selection, explanation or organisation remain? If an expert essay immediately appears, rewrite it.
- **Capability check:** does the model contain genuine ability, not only mistakes?
- **Coherence-of-profile check:** do the weaknesses and successes plausibly belong to one writer?
- **Four-criterion check:** are limitations visible in all four criteria without forcing equal severity or manufacturing a failure in every paragraph?
- **Provenance check:** are original fragments, adaptations, new material and teacher reconstructions clearly distinguished?
- **Task check:** are observations judged against the exact frozen prompt?
- **Feedback check:** does every comment have evidence in the actual response?
- **No-quota check:** have errors emerged plausibly rather than being inserted to reach a target number?
- **Successful-island check:** have useful, controlled passages been retained?
- **Authority check:** are constructed status, estimate limitations and the absence of official approval clear?
- **Upper-rung check:** any estimated Band 6 model must be explicitly constructed and have an evidence-based criterion rationale. Do not present it as authenticated by the current student corpus. Hold models above the authorised estimated Band 6 range.
- **Teaching check:** does the model support noticing, judgement, revision or transfer rather than passive imitation?

Reviewer decisions: **Pass / Revise / Hold**. “Hold” is appropriate when provenance, intended meaning or the stronger evidence needed for a band claim is unavailable. Passing does not authorise deployment.

## 13. Extending the bank when stronger submissions arrive

First inspect the original response, its prompt and any known writing conditions. Separate original writing from later correction. Examine the full four-criterion profile and the basis of any supplied estimate; do not accept the label alone.

Look for stronger task fulfilment, sustained development, generally clear progression and more reliable language control, while retaining actual limitations. Identify which strengths recur and which appear only once. Collect more than one stronger script before treating a style as characteristic of a band.

Document new patterns and add evidence-register entries. Review possible Band 6-and-above anchors against current official descriptors. Use that evidence to review and recalibrate the constructed models and extend the evidence-grounded ladder. Preserve earlier models and their provenance rather than retroactively polishing them.

Pending stronger authentic evidence, the authorised Band 6 constructions are provisional teaching material. Preserve credible limitations and refine their estimates when stronger submissions become available.
