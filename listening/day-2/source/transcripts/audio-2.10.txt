They were part of the Nabataeans’ water-management system over two thousand years ago. [thoughtful] Think about that. Look how dry it is here. They had to bring water into a city and move it to where people needed it. Petra wasn’t built just to be looked at. People lived here. [long pause]

Okay… we’re getting close. [quieter] Keep looking ahead. Not yet… [short pause] There. [long pause] الخزنة. The Treasury. [short pause] I’ve seen this thousands of times… [fascinated] and I still stop! You KNOW it’s there. You’ve SEEN the photographs. But then suddenly… THERE it is! [short pause]

The name is interesting. الخزنة means the Treasury.

Early visitors believed treasure was hidden here, but historians think it was probably… a royal tomb. So the name stayed… [joking] even though the treasure probably didn’t! [laughs] Early morning is BEAUTIFUL for photographs, by the way, when the light enters from the east.. over.. THERE. [short pause] But this is also where I sometimes lose people. Not physically. [long pause] On a clear day, you begin to understand how extensive Petra once was. For me, that’s when it stops feeling like a collection of monuments… and starts feeling like somewhere people lived! People arrive with… uhh… one image in their heads: the Treasury. And yes, it’s magnificent. But Petra wasn’t one building. [thoughtful] They had ordinary lives in a place that, to us, seems extraordinary! That’s what still fascinates me—not just that they carved buildings into rock, but that they made a city here.
